About

I am a researcher in cognitive and systems neuroscience studying the neural mechanisms that support flexible cognition and adaptive behavior in non-human primates.

Research

I study how neural populations dynamically organize, transform, and communicate information to support flexible cognition. My work combines behavioral experiments in macaques, multi-electrode electrophysiology, population-level analyses, and computational modeling. I am particularly interested in how distributed cortical dynamics shape decision-making, internal representations, and behavioral readout.

Methods

Non-human primate behavioral experiments • Multi-electrode electrophysiology • Neural population analysis • Dimensionality reduction • Neural decoding • State-space modeling • Eye tracking • Computational modeling

Current Work

Decision-related population dynamics

I investigate how decision-related signals emerge across neural populations and behavior during evidence accumulation and perceptual decision-making.

Neural and behavioral dynamics

I study the temporal relationship between population activity, eye movements, and spontaneous facial behavior to understand how internal decision variables become expressed in behavior.

Population geometry and cortical communication

I am interested in how the geometry and dynamics of neural population activity determine when information can be transformed, propagated, and read out across cortical networks.
